    Conclusion

    On quality, the desk largely did what it set out to do. QA checked 3,405 claims against 958 sources and found no fabricated source in any of the 68 published posts. Throughput fell short: the goal was at least 4 finished posts a day and the month averaged 2.6, because only 64 of roughly 140 scheduled runs ever started. In the write-up's words, “The design could meet the target. The schedule didn't deliver the runs.”

    What held up

    • Independent QA: it saw only the post, never the author's reasoning, and found a contradicted claim in 31% of published posts.
    • Zero fabricated sources, with 92.6% of sources graded A or B.
    • Rejected posts were published with QA's findings, so the checks were visible.
    • 62 of the 64 runs that started finished cleanly (96.9%).

    What fell short

    • Runs that never started left no logs, so gaps of 2.8 and 5.4 days went unnoticed.
    • A source-checking script crashed in 67 of 74 QA jobs while setup reported success.
    • Four of the six rejections were false “absence” claims, such as saying a paper had no code.
    • The agents filed nine improvement proposals, and none was applied.

    For a second version

    • An external heartbeat that alerts when runs stop.
    • A deterministic script for orchestration.
    • A full-text search tool for checking absence claims.
    • A verdict scale that carries information.
    • A weekly human review slot for proposals.

    HoldingsForecast

    Weekly price forecasts for every stock I hold, from five open-weight forecasting models, each scored against a random walk.

    Read the blog post → GIFT-Eval leaderboard ↗

    Every week, each model reads up to 10 years of Friday closing prices and forecasts each holding 1 week, 3 months and 1 year ahead, as a full 10th-to-90th percentile range rather than a single number. All five run zero-shot, so none is trained on my stocks.

    • Chronos-2Amazon, 120M parameters. The default, with the most mature tooling.
    • TimestralMistral, 209M. Top five among open models on GIFT-Eval.
    • t0The Forecasting Company, about 256M. The top pretrained model on GIFT-Eval.
    • TiRexNX-AI, 35M. Small, and strong on the economics and finance slice.
    • Toto 2.0Datadog, 313M. Built for observability data.

    How it's scored

    • Walk-forward backtest: from 2010, every week, each model sees only the past and forecasts the future.
    • Every model has to beat two baselines: a random walk, and a random walk with drift.
    • Scored on CRPS skill, 80% band coverage, up/down hit rate and how well it ranks the holdings.
    • Results are split by stock and by year, so one lucky stock or one bull market can't carry a model.
    31holdings forecast
    5foundation models + ensemble
    3horizons: 1w, 3m, 1y
    ~25kbacktest forecasts per model

    Hardware

    • A repurposed mining rig: 4× RTX 3060 (12 GB) and 2× RTX 3070 (8 GB) on PCIe x1 risers.
    • One worker per GPU loads each model once; only small chunks of price history cross the risers.
    • A 48-hour budget for the full backtest, with saved predictions so an interrupted run resumes.
