Forecasting

Forecasting a year of USDA meat and poultry production

For my Statistical Machine Learning II final project at Oregon Tech (STAT442, Winter 2025), I took on USDA meat and poultry production statistics. It's a great forecasting dataset because it's long, multiple decades of it, and it's seasonal in a way that's correlated across products. Production of one thing moves with the calendar, and often with production of the others.

The goal was simple to say and harder to do: forecast production as far ahead as possible, and figure out which kind of model actually earns its complexity.

Classic statistics vs. deep learning

I designed and compared two families of models. On one side were regression models, the classic statistical approach, where you can read the coefficients and see why the model thinks what it thinks. On the other were LSTM networks, a type of recurrent neural network built to remember patterns over long sequences. That's exactly the property you want when a seasonal cycle repeats every year for decades.

Running both side by side was the point. Deep learning is the shiny option, but on tabular time series a well-built regression model is often a tough baseline to beat, and comparing them honestly is half of doing data science well.

Pipelines first

Multi-decade data means a lot of cleaning and reshaping before any model sees it, so I automated the data pipelines in Python to make the whole thing run at scale instead of by hand. That paid off twice: re-running experiments became cheap, and the cleaning step itself surfaced something useful.

Results

With about a two-hour training time, the pipeline flagged 1.2% of the data as outliers, and the models produced successful forecasts up to one year in advance.

At a glance

  • Course: STAT442 Statistical Machine Learning II, Oregon Institute of Technology
  • When: Winter 2025
  • Models: LSTM networks and regression models
  • Results: Forecasts up to 1 year ahead; 1.2% of the data flagged as outliers; about 2 hours of training
