Reinforcement Learning

Teaching an agent to play Minesweeper

For Reinforcement Learning (STAT407, Spring 2025), I built an agent that plays Minesweeper. It's a perfect RL playground. The rules are tiny, the board is a grid, and every click is a decision under uncertainty: reveal a safe square and you learn something, guess wrong and the episode is over.

Building the environment

I built a custom OpenAI Gym environment for it. That meant defining what the agent sees (the board as revealed so far), what it can do (pick a square), and how it's rewarded. I optimized the rewards for two things at once: win rate, and solving efficiency, meaning clearing the board without wasted moves.

Q-learning vs. deep RL

I compared tabular Q-learning against deep reinforcement learning methods. The interesting question underneath is exploration versus exploitation: how often should the agent try something new to learn, and how often should it cash in on what it already knows? In Minesweeper, too much exploration means clicking on mines, and too little means never discovering the patterns that make the game solvable.

Q-learning keeps a table of how good each action is in each state, which gets enormous fast on a Minesweeper board. Deep RL replaces that table with a neural network that can generalize between board positions it has never seen.

Results

The deep reinforcement learning strategies won overall. The models converged after about 900 training episodes, with an average of 27 minutes of compute time.

At a glance

  • Course: STAT407 Reinforcement Learning, Oregon Institute of Technology
  • When: Spring 2025
  • Methods: Custom OpenAI Gym environment, Q-learning, deep RL
  • Results: Deep RL won; converged in about 900 episodes at about 27 minutes of compute on average
