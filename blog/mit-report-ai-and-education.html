AI & Education

MIT's report on AI and education, and why it matters beyond the classroom

Originally posted on LinkedIn

https://lnkd.in/p/gv82pjH8

I recently read MIT's new report on AI and Education, and it made me happy to see a strong stance being taken in education. Honestly, it was refreshing. So much of the conversation about AI in schools swings between panic and hype, so a thoughtful middle ground was a welcome change. While the report focuses on education, the same discussion needs to be happening everywhere.

AI capabilities have been advancing faster than many of our policies, standards, and regulations. We need clear opinions more than ever from influential institutions like MIT to help guide what changes are needed most. Right now a lot of people, teachers included, are basically making it up as they go.

As someone who works heavily with AI, software development, and data, as well as having gone through my education during the rise of AI. I've seen firsthand how tumultuous this change has been for my professors, peers, and coworkers. I've watched professors rewrite whole syllabi mid-semester, and I've watched classmates quietly wonder whether the skills they were paying to learn would even matter by graduation. That's a lot of uncertainty to carry around. These changes pose important questions about learning, critical thinking, and long-term skill development.

One passage from the report particularly stood out to me:

"A fundamental danger... is that AI can allow students to bypass learning. Equally concerning is that students may internalize a transactional model in which assignments are outputs, teachers are evaluators, peers are optional, and knowledge (or an MIT degree) is an optimizable commodity to be acquired or produced as efficiently as possible."

Read that last bit again. "An optimizable commodity." Oof.

This is a mindset that I have struggled with my entire life. It is almost a natural instinct to try to 'gamify' life and maximize output without taking the time to think critically about what is really important. I'm a data person, so of course I want to measure everything and find the shortest path. But the shortest path to a grade and the shortest path to actually understanding something are rarely the same road.

Students using AI to complete assignments is simply a symptom of the greater problem. The greater risk is that we begin treating learning as a by-product of output, rather than a goal in and of itself. If an assignment can be fully handed off to a chatbot, that tells us something about the assignment, and maybe about what we've been rewarding all along. It is all too easy to turn life into an optimization problem rather than a developmental process. The true value of education, learning, and development has always been building critical thinking, collaboration, communication, creativity, and the ability to find and solve one's own problems.

MIT's report recommends three key areas of focus:

  • Adapt education for an 'AI-aware' world by redesigning assessments and teaching students how to use AI responsibly.
  • Center people, community, and human connection by emphasizing collaboration, mentorship, discussion, and hands-on experiences.
  • Create systems for continuous reflection and improvement since AI technology is evolving too quickly for static policies.

None of these are flashy, and that's kind of the point. They're about people and process, not just about which tool to ban or allow this semester.

I believe these recommendations apply far beyond universities. The same thinking works on a dev team, in a training program, or around the kitchen table. The challenge isn't whether AI will become part of our daily work and learning. It already has. The challenge is ensuring it provides us with its benefits while negating its downsides. AI should make us sharper, not lazier.

We're all figuring this out in real time, so I'd really love to hear how other people are approaching it. I'll leave you with a simple question, how are you going to educate your children on how to use AI?
