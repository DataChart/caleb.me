Manager or facilitator? What a LinkedIn course taught me about leading

Posted October 3, 2026

Originally posted on LinkedIn

https://lnkd.in/p/gTyR8_gn

I recently completed LinkedIn's course "Facilitation Skills for Managers and Leaders". As someone who constantly finds myself stepping into leadership roles, its great to take a step away from the keyboard and work on an area where I still have a lot to learn. Real talk: I can debug a pipeline at 2 a.m., but getting a room full of people to agree on one is a whole different skill.

The course emphasized that good leaders need to move between being a manager and being a facilitator. While both roles are important, they serve very different purposes. I'll admit I'd been blurring the two together for years without really noticing.

As a manager, you're focused on outcomes. You establish goals, define priorities, set expectations, track progress, and help your team deliver on their commitments. This is the part most of us are comfortable with, because it's easy to measure.

As a facilitator, you're focused on the process. Your role is to create a safe environment for discussion, encourage participation, recognize the strengths of your team, empower ownership, and help people work together toward a common goal. It's a lot less about you talking and a lot more about everyone else getting a word in.

What resonated with me most is that good leadership requires switching between these two mindsets. First, you help establish where the team is going. Then, you help the team figure out how to get there. The trick is knowing which hat you're wearing at any given moment.

The course also introduced two simple questions that I think every leader should regularly ask themselves:

  • Does everyone understand the goal?
  • Does everyone have the information they need?

They sound almost too simple, right? But when a project goes sideways, one of them is usually a quiet "no."

Another lesson that stood out to me is that good facilitation isn't about having all the answers. It's about listening carefully, asking good questions, connecting people to the resources they need, and helping the team uncover solutions together. Honestly, that one stung a little (in a good way).

Whether you're managing a project, leading a meeting, or guiding a team through change, the best leaders seem to balance structure with flexibility. They know how to resolve conflict, adapt when circumstances change, and avoid the temptation to micromanage. Micromanaging is sneaky, too. It usually starts with good intentions.

As I continue leading teams towards success, I'm going to focus more on finding and creating the conditions that allow others to succeed. In practice that means more questions, more listening, and fewer moments where I just grab the keyboard and fix it myself.

Anyway, that's my big takeaway. What's one leadership lesson keeps surprising you? Let me know on LinkedIn!
