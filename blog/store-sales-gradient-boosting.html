Forecasting

Store sales forecasting with gradient boosting, delivered to your inbox

For Regression and Time Series (STAT412, Spring 2025), I built forecasting models for retail store sales. Retail data is high-dimensional in the most annoying way: lots of stores, lots of products, holidays, promotions, and every one of them nudging the numbers.

Why gradient boosting

I used XGBoost as the main model. Gradient boosting builds a long chain of small decision trees, where each new tree focuses on the mistakes of the ones before it. It handles lots of features and messy interactions well, which is exactly what retail data throws at you.

To keep myself honest, I built dynamic visual dashboards that compared XGBoost against other gradient boosting methods like LightGBM, so it was easy to see where each one did better.

From model to something people use

Then came the last mile. I automated the evaluation metrics and dashboards so the forecasts were emailed directly to the user. Nobody had to open a notebook or rerun anything. In total, that saved 10 hours of work per week.

Results

The models achieved high predictive performance for metrics up to one week in advance.

At a glance

  • When: Spring 2025
  • Models: XGBoost, compared with LightGBM and other gradient boosting methods
  • Results: Strong forecasts up to 1 week ahead; automated forecast emails saved 10 hours a week
